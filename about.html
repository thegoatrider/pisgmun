A. About the United Nations

The United Nations (UN) is an international organisation founded in 1945 in the aftermath of World War II. It began with 51 countries collaborating to create a platform dedicated to maintaining global peace and stability. Over the years, more nations have joined, and today the UN boasts 193 member states. Its headquarters are located in New York City, USA.

Key UN Organs & Bodies:

1. General Assembly (UNGA)

Main deliberative body of the UN where all 193 member states discuss and coordinate on international issues and policies.

2. Security Council (UNSC)

Responsible for maintaining international peace and security, consisting of 15 members including 5 permanent ones (P5).

3. Economic & Social Council (ECOSOC)

Coordinates economic, social, and environmental work, as well as global policy recommendations.

4. Trusteeship Council

Originally established to oversee trust territories; currently inactive since all territories gained self-governance.

5. International Court of Justice (ICJ)

Settles legal disputes between sovereign countries and provides advisory opinions on international law.

6. UN Secretariat

Carries out the day-to-day administrative work of the UN, headed by the Secretary-General.

C. Model United Nations (MUN) & Its Purpose

MUN is an educational simulation that replicates the process of an actual UN conference. It provides students an invaluable opportunity to take on the role of a UN delegate and acquire skills such as conflict management and global cooperation.

What does PMUN offer students?

  • A chance to explore real global issues through research, dialogue, and diplomacy.
  • A platform to practise leadership, negotiation, and teamwork.
  • Builds problem-solving, decision-making, and collaborative skills.
  • Encourages students to think deeply and speak confidently.

Why should a school conduct MUN?

  • Thought Leadership: Demonstrates commitment to global education.
  • Prestige & Visibility: Attracts attention from educators and peer schools.
  • Student Leadership: Roles for student organizers and chairs.
